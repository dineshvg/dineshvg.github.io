09 · Leadership and behavioral

status: todo · priority P1 · 3h · verified 2026-11-16

Lead and architect roles are hired on judgment and influence; your stories are the evidence.

Why it comes up

  • Behavioral rounds probe how you decide, disagree, mentor and recover, usually with "tell me about a time".

Know cold

  • Story structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result, and what you would do differently. Two minutes each.
  • Themes to cover (see the story bank, which is personal): architecture decision with trade-offs; an agent was wrong and you caught it; leading a migration; disagreement; incident or release problem; security trade-off; mentoring and raising standards; a tool you built yourself.
  • AI adoption on a team: your stance on introducing agents (pilot, policy, measurement, review standards) is a likely lead-level question; connect it to module 01.
  • Architect habits: decisions recorded as ADRs, quality goals named, trade-offs stated, reviews that teach rather than gatekeep.

Likely questions

Question What a strong answer contains
Tell me about an architecture decision you got wrong. Honest account, what signal you missed, what changed.
How do you handle a disagreement with a senior peer? Shared goal, data, options with trade-offs, decision rule, how you disagreed and committed.
How do you raise code quality across a team? Standards in tooling, review culture, examples, measured outcomes.
How would you introduce coding agents to a team? Pilot, guardrails, verification standards, metrics, handling skeptics.
Describe a production incident you led. Detection, mitigation, root cause, follow-up changes.
How do you mentor? A specific person and outcome, not a philosophy.

Practice

  • ☐ Fill at least six stories in the story bank (kept private) with numbers.
  • ☐ Record each story aloud once; trim to two minutes.
  • ☐ Prepare a 60-second answer to "why this role, why now".
